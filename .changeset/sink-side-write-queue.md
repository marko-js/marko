Route observable DOM writes through runtime sinks gated by a branch write-state machine, in support of async transitions. Every sink now takes the target's scope and accessor first (`_text(scope, accessor, value)`) and resolves its node internally, so held writes key on `(scope, sink, accessor[, name])` and re-resolve their target when they finally apply. Sinks stay raw until `_enable_transition()` — emitted once per template, only when an `<await>` promise expression has referenced bindings and can re-fire client side — swaps them for wrappers that dispatch on the write state of the target's branch: attached content joins an atomic flush-end commit batch; content in created (not yet inserted) or detached branches is unobservable, so writes apply immediately to the off-document DOM; content under a pending transition's visible freeze is captured in a per-region store with latest-args-wins overwrite, one entry per target. State transitions ride the existing branch primitives — creation stamps created, insertion adopts the parent branch's state, a transition freeze flips its region to the hold store, and detaching drains the store onto the now-hidden DOM before stamping detached, so a held write can never clobber a later direct one and a hold spanning any number of flushes applies at most one mutation per target. Structural swaps and loop reconciles keep single-phase direct drivers until the same enable installs their two-phase form (eager bookkeeping, queued DOM phase, superseded never-inserted branches destroyed without DOM churn). Apps without client-refiring awaits keep raw sink calls and direct structural drivers and tree-shake the entire subsystem away.
